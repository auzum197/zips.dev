#Terminology

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", and "SHOULD" in this document are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14 1 when, and only when, they appear in all capitals.

The terms "block chain", "consensus branch", and "network upgrade" follow ZIP 200. 2 The character § refers to sections of the Zcash Protocol Specification. 3

"Block target spacing" means the interval between blocks targeted by the difficulty adjustment algorithm. Actual block intervals vary.

The "25-second baseline" means the rules after NU7, including ZIP 218's 25-second target spacing and ZIP 237's subsidy definitions. 4 5

An "orphan block" is a valid mined block that does not remain on the selected chain. This document reports "orphan blocks per accepted block" separately from the fraction of all mined blocks that become orphaned.

"Reach 90%" means the time for a block to reach 90% of the specified node sample. "Effective old-tip mining time" means the accumulated mining work spent on the old tip, expressed as seconds of the whole network's mining power.

MB means 1,000,000 bytes.

#Abstract

This proposal reduces block target spacing from 25 to 20 seconds in a network upgrade after NU7. It builds on ZIP 218 and lowers its per-block shielded action limits and global shielded budget by 20%, from 330 to 264 Orchard-protocol actions and from 300 to 240 Sapling inputs and outputs. 4 6 The block size limit is unchanged.

The change reduces the expected wait for a first confirmation by 20%. Shielded throughput, worst-case light wallet bandwidth, and trial decryptions per day are unchanged, apart from 25% more block headers. It adjusts the difficulty averaging window, issuance parameters, and duration-based node defaults to the new spacing. Shorter inclusion intervals can expose finer transaction timing information.

#Motivation

A 20-second target reduces the expected wait for a first confirmation by five seconds. ZIP 218 chose its shielded action limits to bound worst-case light wallet sync bandwidth and trial decryptions per day. 4 Scaling those limits with the target spacing keeps these bounds, so mobile and other light wallets pay only for the additional block headers. This proposal evaluates that incremental trade-off from ZIP 218's baseline.

#Privacy Implications

ZIP 218 identifies timing and anchor-depth privacy effects. 4 Shorter blocks give observers finer timing information without changing what individual shielded transactions reveal.

A three-block anchor depth spans an expected 60 seconds instead of 75. A rollback past the anchor invalidates the transaction. A replacement spending the same notes exposes the same nullifiers, linking the two transactions. Ordinary orphan-block measurements do not bound rollback depth.

The smaller per-block action limits do not change the maximum shielded throughput per unit time. Under sustained demand for a pool, ZIP 218's observation that the limits can delay transactions and make their timing more distinguishable continues to apply. 4

#Requirements

  • Reduce target spacing from 25 to 20 seconds after NU7.
  • Preserve approximately the difficulty averaging duration and issuance schedule, including partial halving intervals, subject to integer rounding.
  • Preserve ZIP 218's worst-case shielded throughput, compact sync bandwidth for shielded data, and trial decryptions per day.
  • Preserve historical consensus rules and integrate the transition with ZIP 237 and active funding-stream schedules.

#Transactions exceeding a block limit at activation

A transaction that by itself would violate any of these limits cannot be included in any block at a height h≥A20. This includes transactions that satisfy ZIP 218's limits, such as one with 300 Orchard actions.

Nodes SHOULD NOT admit such a transaction to their mempool, or relay it, if the candidate next block's height is at or above A20. Nodes SHOULD evict such transactions from their mempool when the candidate next block's height reaches A20. Wallets SHOULD NOT create such a transaction for a target height at or above A20.

#Minimum difficulty blocks on Testnet

Retain ZIP 218's threshold of 18⋅𝖯𝗈𝖶𝖳𝖺𝗋𝗀𝖾𝗍𝖲𝗉𝖺𝖼𝗂𝗇𝗀(h) seconds, evaluated with the new spacing. At and after A20, the threshold is therefore 18×20=360 seconds, rather than ZIP 218's 450 seconds. A block qualifies only if its timestamp is strictly more than this threshold after the preceding block's timestamp. Thus a gap of 360 seconds does not qualify, while a gap of 361 seconds does. In that case the block's nBits MUST equal 𝖳𝗈𝖢𝗈𝗆𝗉𝖺𝖼𝗍(𝖯𝗈𝖶𝖫𝗂𝗆𝗂𝗍), following ZIP 218. This rule does not affect Mainnet.

Existing timestamp-validity bounds and local-clock tolerances remain unchanged. The Testnet minimum-difficulty exception remains subject to those rules.

#Transaction expiry

Wallets SHOULD increase the default expiry delta from 120 to 150 blocks. Choose the default using the transaction's target height, which is the next block height after the chain tip. Use 150 when that height is at or above A20; otherwise retain the prior default. Compute expiry relative to the target height as specified by ZIP 218. Wallets SHOULD preserve an explicit user-configured delta across activation.

#Rationale

#Capacity and resource costs

ZIP 218 chose its shielded action limits so that worst-case light wallet sync bandwidth does not increase relative to the pre-NU7 protocol. 4 Retaining those per-block limits at 20 seconds would raise the maximum number of shielded actions per day, and with it worst-case compact sync bandwidth and trial decryptions, by 25%. This proposal instead scales the limits by 4/5, so the daily maxima are unchanged. All three ZIP 218 values are multiples of 5, so no rounding is needed.

Using ZIP 218's compact-field model, which excludes serialization and transport overhead, with 86,400/20=4,320 blocks per day:

The binding constraint remains a block filled with Orchard or Ironwood actions: 264×148×4,320+90×4,320=169.18 𝖬𝖡/𝖽𝖺𝗒. The only increase is the 25% more compact block headers, approximately 78 kB per day. The trial decryption count, counting both internal and external incoming viewing keys as in ZIP 218, is 2×264×4,320=2,280,960 per account per day, identical to ZIP 218.

These figures describe worst-case compact sync bandwidth when blocks fill the shielded action budget. They do not establish a daily download requirement for every wallet.

Maximum shielded throughput is also unchanged. Two-action Orchard or Ironwood transactions remain limited to ⌊264/2⌋/20=6.6 transactions per second, and four-IO Sapling transactions to ⌊240/4⌋/20=3.0 transactions per second, as in ZIP 218. The benefit of this proposal is lower latency, not more shielded capacity.

The worst-case shielded verification work per block falls by 20%, while the worst-case shielded verification work per unit time is approximately unchanged. Batch verification amortizes some cost across a block, so slightly smaller blocks may verify somewhat less efficiently per action. Fixed per-block costs, such as header validation, chain state updates, and propagation round trips, increase by 25% per unit time. Syncing blocks produced during a given time offline requires processing 25% more headers.

The 2 MB block size limit is unchanged, so the maximum transparent data per unit time increases by 25%. This affects full node bandwidth and storage, but not light wallets that download only compact blocks.

#Orphan blocks and effective propagation time

With ZIP-218, there was measured work to evaluate the orphan rate, and re-org rate in highly distributed testnets with full 2MB blocks. We repeat that methodology, using 2 action Ironwood transactions until the original ZIP-218 limit, and then transparent transactions afterwards. These distributed networks contain 80 equal-weighted miners, evenly spread across 12 regions (including US-east, US-west, Australia, Amsterdam, London, Frankfurt, Bangalore, Singapore). 4 8

With 20s full blocks, with an all Zakura node network work as above, we measured 1.9% orphan rates. This was using the older ZIP-218 action count, so thereby having a higher verification load due to the increased number of ZKP's. For reference this value is currently 1.45% on Zakura {TODO: Update this old benchmark to latest}.

Since NU7 went live, we've seen orphan rate increase from 0.66% to 0.83%, albeit block utilization is not constant in these time periods. The 0.66% orphan rate is measured from a time period where blocks were on average 53kb large, whereas the 0.85% is from a time period of 10kb on average.

#Difficulty averaging duration

ZIP 218's 102-block window at 25 seconds spans an expected 2,550 seconds. The equivalent count at 20 seconds is 127.5 blocks. This proposal rounds up to 128, giving 2,560 seconds, about 0.39% longer.

This proposal preserves the averaging duration because mining hash rate can vary. Reducing that duration would change the DAA's response to hash rate changes as well as changing block target spacing.

#Issuance and schedule continuity

The interval change preserves its expected duration:

6,300,000×20=5,040,000×25 seconds.

The halving formula retains progress through the 25-second era. Scaling the unrounded scheduled subsidy by 4/5 preserves the expected issuance rate before rounding. Integer rounding changes the realized schedule.

The NSM fraction changes from 1375/1010 to 1100/1010, also a factor of 4/5. ZIP 237's ceiling and evolving balance mean this does not establish exact equality of reissuance per unit of wall-clock time.

Open specification item: amend the funding-stream end heights and address period calculations in ZIPs 207 and 214 for a second spacing transition. 9 10 Preserve fractional progress through the active address period and define integer-boundary rounding. Keep ended streams ended and ensure recipient lists cover every extended period. The deployment height and schedule amendments must be settled together; changing NU7 constants retroactively is invalid.

#Deployment

Deploy this proposal in a separate network upgrade after NU7. ZIP 259 defines NU7's deployment; this proposal does not replace that deployment. 6 A separate deployment ZIP will assign this upgrade's activation heights and consensus branch IDs.

Before this ZIP advances to Proposed status, resolve the funding-stream and NSM reissuance-height specification items. Complete the activation and rounding analysis against the selected deployment parameters.
