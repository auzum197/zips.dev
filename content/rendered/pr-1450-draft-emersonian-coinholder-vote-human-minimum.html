#Terminology

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "SHOULD", and "MAY" in this document are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14 1 when, and only when, they appear in all capitals.

The terms "Mainnet" and "Testnet" in this document are to be interpreted as defined in the Zcash protocol specification 2.

The terms "Coinholder-Controlled Fund" and "Key-Holder Organizations" in this document are to be interpreted as described in ZIP 1016 3, as amended by any ZIP that updates the membership of the Key-Holder Organizations (see for example 4).

"ZEC" refers to the native currency of Zcash on Mainnet.

The terms below are to be interpreted as follows:

Coinholder-amount voting round
Any coinholder vote held under ZIP 1016 5, in which each ballot is weighted by the amount of ZEC that the voter proves control of. This includes votes on grant applications, separate votes on competing proposals, and additional votes on whether funding of an existing grant should continue.
Coinholder poll
A coinholder-amount voting round, or any other poll of ZEC holders that is organized, administered, or certified by one or more Key-Holder Organizations, or that a ZIP cites as evidence of coinholder support or opposition.
Ballot
A single vote cast in a coinholder poll on a single question. A ballot is one of Approve, Reject, or Abstain (or the equivalent options of a poll that is not a yes/no question).
ZEC voted
ZEC voted in a coinholder poll, for a given question, is the total amount of ZEC committed by all ballots cast on that question, including ballots that Abstain.
Verified coinholder-person
A natural person who, for a given coinholder poll, has been verified under the Human Verification Methodology in effect for that poll as (a) being a unique natural person, and (b) controlling at least the Minimum Personhood Balance of ZEC at the snapshot height used for that poll.
Human Verification Methodology
The document described in Human Verification Methodology.
Personhood Attestation
The Personhood Attestation for a coinholder poll is the report described in Personhood Attestation.

#Abstract

This ZIP updates ZIP 1016 3 so that a coinholder-amount voting round can only approve a grant, or otherwise bind the Key-Holder Organizations, if:

  • the ZEC threshold of ZIP 1016 is met, as it stands or as raised by the companion quorum draft 6;
  • ballots were cast on the question by at least 50 distinct verified coinholder-persons; and
  • the Key-Holder Organizations published their Human Verification Methodology in advance, and publish a Personhood Attestation for the result.

It requires the Key-Holder Organizations to publish the methodology they use to verify that participants are distinct humans, in a way that preserves the privacy of voters and of their vote choices. A coinholder poll that does not meet these conditions is advisory only.

This ZIP does not change how ballots are weighted. Coinholder-amount voting rounds continue to be decided by the amount of ZEC voted. The human participation requirement is a floor on legitimacy, not a second vote.

#Motivation

ZIP 1016 requires a minimum amount of ZEC to be voted, with a simple majority in favor, for a grant proposal to be approved 5. That threshold counts coins, never people. As of late 2026, the Grayscale Zcash ETF alone held about 594,293 ZEC 7, more than the whole 420,000 ZEC threshold, and Cypherpunk Technologies about 326,417 ZEC 8. A single holder could therefore make a vote valid with no one else taking part. The author does not claim that anyone has done so. The concern is that a result alone cannot show that coinholders in general, rather than one balance sheet, supported a grant.

The companion quorum draft 6 raises the threshold to 1,000,000 ZEC, but any coin threshold can be met by whoever accumulates enough coins. This ZIP therefore also requires a minimum number of distinct humans to have taken part.

Counting humans requires a method for verifying personhood. Today no ZIP defines "coinholder" 3, and the voting model that ZIP 1016 says will be "specified in another ZIP [TBD]" 9 has not been written. The Key-Holder Organizations already organize and certify votes, and so act as de facto election administrators. If they are to count humans, the community needs to be able to see and critique how they do it, before the vote rather than after.

Zcash's purpose is financial privacy. Any proof-of-personhood process for Zcash coinholder polls must therefore be designed so that verifying that a voter is a unique human does not reveal who that voter is, which shielded addresses they control, or how they voted.

#Privacy Implications

Counting distinct humans means that someone has to check personhood, and any such check can leak information. A careless process could link a voter's legal identity to their shielded holdings, reveal which addresses or notes they control, or record how they voted. Any of these would undermine the financial privacy that Zcash exists to provide, and could expose voters to coercion or targeting.

This ZIP does not choose a proof-of-personhood technology. Instead, the Specification limits what a Human Verification Methodology may require and what may be published. Participants do not have to publish their legal identity, move funds out of a shielded pool, or reveal a shielded address or viewing key, and no link between a person and their ballot is recorded. The Personhood Attestation reports counts only. The methodology also has to say what data is collected, who sees it, and how long it is kept, so that the community can judge the remaining privacy risk before each vote.

Taking part in personhood verification is optional. A coinholder who does not want to be verified can still vote, and their ZEC is counted as before.

#Requirements

  • A coinholder-amount voting round cannot bind the Key-Holder Organizations unless a minimum number of distinct humans participated.
  • The method by which humans are counted is published before voting begins, and cannot be changed during a voting window.
  • The process does not require publication of any voter's identity, the addresses they control, or the link between a person and their ballot.
  • The outcome of every coinholder poll states whether it met the conditions to be binding, so that the community can tell a binding result from an advisory one.

#Non-requirements

  • This ZIP does not introduce person-weighted (one-person-one-vote) decision making, and does not change the weighting of ballots by ZEC amount.
  • This ZIP does not specify a particular proof-of-personhood technology. Choosing one is left to the Key-Holder Organizations, subject to the publication, privacy, and audit requirements below.
  • This ZIP does not change the ZEC threshold of ZIP 1016. That is the subject of the companion quorum draft 6.
  • This ZIP does not change the veto process of ZIP 1016 10, the funding streams, or the membership of the Key-Holder Organizations.
  • This ZIP does not specify the general voting model that ZIP 1016 defers to "another ZIP [TBD]", although it constrains any such model.

#Specification

#Changes to ZIP 1016

In the "Disbursement process" section of ZIP 1016 5, the following sentence is added after the sentence that sets the minimum amount of ZEC to be voted:

In addition, ballots on a grant proposal MUST have been cast by at least 50 distinct verified coinholder-persons, and the other conditions in ZIP [this ZIP] MUST be met, for the grant proposal to be approved.

In the "Testnet-specific considerations" section of ZIP 1016, the following sentence is added after the sentence about the Mainnet ZEC threshold: "The minimum number of verified coinholder-persons applied to Mainnet coinholder votes does not apply to these rehearsals, but Testnet rehearsals SHOULD exercise the Human Verification Methodology."

#Minimum human participation

A question in a coinholder-amount voting round MUST NOT be treated as approved unless ballots on that question were cast by at least 50 distinct verified coinholder-persons. Ballots that Abstain count toward this minimum.

Each verified coinholder-person counts at most once toward this minimum for a given question, regardless of how many addresses, wallets, or ballots they use.

A verified coinholder-person counts toward this minimum regardless of how much ZEC they hold above the Minimum Personhood Balance, and regardless of which way they voted.

Coinholders who do not take part in personhood verification MAY still vote. Their ZEC counts toward ZEC voted and toward the majority as before. They do not count toward the minimum number of verified coinholder-persons.

#Human Verification Methodology

The Key-Holder Organizations MUST jointly publish a Human Verification Methodology, and MUST publish it on the Zcash Community Forum and at a stable URL linked from the announcement of each coinholder poll.

The Human Verification Methodology for a coinholder poll MUST be published at least 30 days before the voting window for that poll opens, matching the existing 30-day community review period for grant applications 5. It MUST NOT be changed between that publication and the publication of the Personhood Attestation for that poll, except to correct a security vulnerability, in which case the change and the reason for it MUST be published immediately.

The Human Verification Methodology MUST describe at least:

  1. Verification method: how a participant demonstrates that they are a unique natural person, including any third-party services, credentials, in-person events, social attestation, or cryptographic protocols used.
  2. Uniqueness: how the process prevents one person from being counted more than once for the same question (Sybil resistance), and the known limits of that protection.
  3. Coinholder link: how a verified person is linked to control of at least the Minimum Personhood Balance of ZEC at the poll's snapshot height, without revealing which addresses or notes they control.
  4. Minimum Personhood Balance: the minimum amount of ZEC that a person must control to be counted as a verified coinholder-person. This MUST be greater than zero, and SHOULD be no more than 1 ZEC.
  5. Privacy: what data is collected about participants, who can see it, how the link between a person and their ballot is kept from being recorded or disclosed, and how long any data is retained.
  6. Accessibility: how a coinholder can take part regardless of jurisdiction, wallet software, or whether they hold ZEC in shielded or transparent addresses, and what alternatives exist for those who cannot use the primary method.
  7. Administration: which Key-Holder Organization or contracted third party operates each part of the process, and any conflicts of interest they have in the poll's outcome.
  8. Audit: how an independent party, or the public, can check the count of verified coinholder-persons without learning their identities.
  9. Disputes: how a participant, applicant, or member of the community can challenge a verification decision or the published count, and who decides.

The Human Verification Methodology:

  • MUST NOT require that a participant's legal identity be published, or be disclosed to any party other than one named in the methodology under item 7.
  • MUST NOT record or publish any link between a verified coinholder-person and the content of their ballot.
  • MUST NOT require that a participant move funds out of a shielded pool, or reveal a shielded address or viewing key to any party.
  • SHOULD prefer methods in which no single party learns both a participant's identity and the fact that they took part in the poll.
  • SHOULD be the same for every question in a voting window.

Any Key-Holder Organization MAY additionally publish its own objections to, or reservations about, a jointly published methodology.

#Personhood Attestation

Within 7 days after a voting window closes, and before any disbursement is signed on the basis of a vote held in that window, the Key-Holder Organizations MUST publish a Personhood Attestation that states, for each question:

  • the ZEC voted, the ZEC in favor, the ZEC against, and the ZEC abstaining (ZEC voted includes abstaining ZEC);
  • the number of distinct verified coinholder-persons who cast a ballot on it;
  • whether each of the conditions in this ZIP was met; and
  • a reference to the Human Verification Methodology that was in effect.

The Personhood Attestation MUST be signed by each Key-Holder Organization that agrees with it. A Key-Holder Organization that does not agree with the attestation MUST publish its reasons.

The Personhood Attestation MUST NOT contain any information that identifies a verified coinholder-person or links them to their ballot.

#Binding and advisory results

A coinholder poll is "binding" only if:

  1. a Human Verification Methodology was published for it as required above;
  2. the minimum amount of ZEC required by ZIP 1016, as amended at the time the voting window opens, was voted on the question;
  3. ballots on the question were cast by at least 50 distinct verified coinholder-persons; and
  4. a Personhood Attestation for it has been published, and is signed by at least two Key-Holder Organizations.

A coinholder poll that is not binding is "advisory". In particular:

  • The Key-Holder Organizations MUST NOT sign a disbursement from the Coinholder-Controlled Fund on the basis of a coinholder-amount voting round that is not binding. A grant proposal on which the vote is advisory is not approved, whatever the outcome of the ballots.
  • Where a vote on whether funding of an existing grant should continue is advisory, the existing grant continues on its existing terms, as if the vote had not been held.
  • A ZIP or Key-Holder Organization communication MUST NOT describe the result of an advisory coinholder poll as a decision, approval, or mandate of coinholders. It MAY report the result, labeled as advisory, with the Personhood Attestation.

The Key-Holder Organizations' veto rights under ZIP 1016 10 are unchanged and apply only to binding votes.

#Scope

This ZIP applies to every coinholder-amount voting round under ZIP 1016 3, including separate votes on competing proposals and votes on the continuation of an existing grant.

For a coinholder poll that is not a coinholder-amount voting round, such as a sentiment poll on a protocol change, this ZIP does not decide what weight the community gives the result. The poll's organizers SHOULD apply the Human Verification Methodology, and the result MUST be reported as binding or advisory under the rules above.

#Activation

This ZIP takes effect for any coinholder poll whose voting window opens on or after the later of:

  • the date this ZIP's Status becomes Active; and
  • 30 days after the first Human Verification Methodology is published.

Until then, coinholder-amount voting rounds continue under the existing rules of ZIP 1016.

#Rationale

#Why a human minimum rather than human-weighted votes

Changing to one-person-one-vote would be a larger change to ZIP 1016's funding model, which was adopted on the basis of giving coinholders a voice in proportion to their holdings. A minimum number of distinct participants keeps coin-weighted decisions, but requires evidence that a grant was approved by more than a handful of parties. It is a quorum of people, not a vote of people.

Parallel person-based polls, such as the coinperson poll planned for December 2026 11, can inform whether a stronger, bicameral requirement is warranted in a future ZIP.

#Why 50 humans

50 is low enough that any genuinely community-supported proposal should be able to reach it, and high enough that a single holder, or a small group of insiders, cannot reach it without visibly recruiting others. The number of distinct participants in past rounds has not been published, so the threshold can be revisited once Personhood Attestations provide data.

#Why the Key-Holder Organizations publish the methodology

The Key-Holder Organizations already agree the grant application process, decide which proposals are in competition, and sign disbursements 5. They are the parties who would act on a personhood count, and are the natural parties to be accountable for it. Requiring publication before the voting window opens means that the method can be reviewed alongside the grant applications, and cannot be adjusted after the fact to change an outcome.

The ZIP sets requirements on what the methodology must disclose and protect, rather than choosing a technology, because proof-of-personhood methods are changing quickly and each has different privacy and accessibility trade-offs.

#Why privacy constraints

A proof-of-personhood process that links identities to shielded holdings or to ballots would undermine the financial privacy that Zcash exists to provide, and could expose voters to coercion. The constraints in this ZIP are intended to make the count of humans checkable without making any individual human visible.

#Open questions

  • Should this ZIP stay separate from the quorum draft 6, or be merged with it after community polling?
  • Should the human minimum scale with the size of the grant, for example requiring more verified coinholder-persons for grants above a given amount?
  • Should the Minimum Personhood Balance be fixed in this ZIP rather than set by the methodology?
  • Should the Personhood Attestation require all of the Key-Holder Organizations, rather than two?
  • Should Zcash Community Grants, or another party independent of the Key-Holder Organizations, audit the Personhood Attestation?
  • ZIP 1016 only recommends that the Key-Holder Organizations sign the transactions for an approved grant. Should signing be made mandatory for binding votes?
