#Terminology

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "SHALL", "SHOULD", and "MAY" in this document are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14 1 when, and only when, they appear in all capitals.

The terms "Zcash Community Grants" (or "ZCG"), "ZCG Committee", "Financial Privacy Foundation" (or "FPF"), "Zcash Community Advisory Panel" (or "ZCAP"), and "Discretionary Budget" in this document are to be interpreted as defined or used in ZIP 1015 2.

The terms "Bootstrap Project" (or "BP"), "Electric Coin Company" (or "ECC"), and "Zcash Foundation" (or "ZF") in this document are to be interpreted as used in ZIP 1015 2.

"Zcash Open Development Lab" (or "ZODL") refers to Znewco, Inc., the company formed in 2026 by the former engineering and product team of ECC 3. ZODL is a separate organization from ECC.

The term "Key-Holder Organizations" in this document is to be interpreted as described in ZIP 1016 4, as amended by any ZIP that updates their membership (see for example 3).

"ZEC" refers to the native currency of Zcash on Mainnet.

The terms below are to be interpreted as follows:

Member
A person who holds a seat on the ZCG Committee.
Seated Members
The Members holding a seat at a given time. A seat that is vacant is not counted.
Large Grant
A grant, or an extension, renewal, or increase of a grant, whose total value over its full term is more than US$500,000, valued at the time of the decision. Grants to the same applicant for closely related work approved within a 90-day period are added together for this purpose.

#Abstract

This ZIP increases the ZCG Committee from five seats to seven.

The two new seats are filled by the same process that selects the existing seats, in the next selection round after this ZIP becomes Active, and are placed in the election cycles so that four seats and three seats come up for selection at different times. The association limits in ZIP 1015 are extended to ZODL and to the Key-Holder Organizations, and at most three Members in total may have any of those associations, so that at least four Members have none.

The ZIP also sets a quorum of five Members, and requires at least four votes in favor for any Large Grant, so that a decision worth more than US$500,000 is always backed by a majority of the full Committee.

Committee compensation continues to be set by ZCAP and paid from the Discretionary Budget. The ZIP has no significant privacy implications.

#Motivation

ZIP 1015 provides that "ZCG awards are subject to approval by a five-seat ZCG Committee" 5. That size was carried over from the Major Grants committee of ZIP 1014 6, when the amounts involved were much smaller and ZCG's role was narrower. ZIP 1016 recognized this, noting that "Any changes to the ZCG or its governance, such as its expansion to include more members, may be desired but are specifically outside this proposal's scope" 4.

The ZCG funding stream is 8% of the block subsidy. At the current issuance rate that is about 144 ZEC per day, or roughly 52,500 ZEC a year (8% of 1.5625 ZEC per 75-second block, or the equivalent at 25-second spacing). At recent prices this is on the order of US$60 million a year, decided by five people. These figures are the author's arithmetic and are given for scale only.

A five-seat Committee has several weaknesses at that scale:

  • Recusal removes a large share of the Committee. ZIP 1015 requires Members to recuse when they have a financial interest. One recusal leaves four people, and two leave three, so a single conflicted decision can rest on a bare majority of a much smaller group.
  • The association limits leave little room for independence. ZIP 1015 allows at most one Member associated with each of BP/ECC, ZF, and FPF 5. On a five-seat Committee, those three seats can form a majority on their own. ZIP 1015 also says nothing about ZODL, which did not exist when it was written, or about the Key-Holder Organizations, which administer the Coinholder-Controlled Fund.
  • A majority is easy to reach. Three people decide any question, and three people are a small target for pressure from applicants or other interests.
  • Workload is concentrated. Five people review every grant, monitor milestones, and approve Discretionary Budget spending, which limits how carefully each application can be read.

Seven seats address these without changing how the Committee is selected or what it does.

#Privacy Implications

This ZIP has no significant privacy implications. It changes the number of seats on an existing public committee and its voting rules. The new Members are selected and disclosed in the same way as current Members.

#Requirements

  • The ZCG Committee has seven seats.
  • The new seats are filled by the existing selection process, without interrupting the Committee's work.
  • Seats continue to come up for selection on a staggered schedule.
  • Once the new seats are filled, at least four Members have no association with BP/ECC, ZODL, ZF, FPF, or any Key-Holder Organization.
  • Once the new seats are filled, decisions are normally made by at least five Members and never by fewer than four, and no Large Grant is approved with fewer than four votes in favor.
  • The cost of the larger Committee stays within the existing Discretionary Budget process.

#Non-requirements

  • This ZIP does not change who selects the ZCG Committee, the one-year term, or the conflict-of-interest policy in ZIP 1015.
  • This ZIP does not add a procedure for removing Members or filling mid-term vacancies. Those are left to other proposals.
  • This ZIP does not change the ZCG funding stream or the uses of ZCG funds.

#Specification

#Number of seats

In item 5 of the section "Zcash Community Grants (ZCG)" of ZIP 1015 5, "a five-seat ZCG Committee" is replaced with "a seven-seat ZCG Committee".

#Association limits

In item 7 of the section "Zcash Community Grants (ZCG)" of ZIP 1015 5, the sentence beginning "At most one person with association with the BP/ECC" is replaced with:

At most one person with association with the BP/ECC or ZODL, at most one person with association with the ZF, at most one person with association with FPF, and at most one person with association with each other Key-Holder Organization are allowed to sit on the ZCG Committee. In total, at most three Members may have an association with any of these organizations, so that at least four Members of a seven-seat Committee have none.

The definition of "Association" in that item applies to each of these organizations.

These limits apply to selections made after this ZIP becomes Active. A Member already seated when this ZIP becomes Active MAY serve out their current term.

#Filling the new seats

The two new seats SHALL be filled by the ZF's Community Advisory Panel or successor process, in the same way as the existing seats, at the first selection round that opens at least 30 days after this ZIP becomes Active. If no selection round is scheduled within 90 days after this ZIP becomes Active, the selecting body SHOULD hold a special round for the new seats within that period.

To keep elections staggered, the selecting body SHALL assign the seven seats to two groups, one of four seats and one of three, whose selection rounds are held at different times of the year. Where this requires one of the new seats to be filled for a first term shorter or longer than one year, that first term MAY be adjusted by up to six months. Every later term is one year, as in item 7 of the section "Zcash Community Grants (ZCG)" of ZIP 1015.

The selecting body SHALL publish the assignment of seats to groups, and the dates of each group's selection round, on the Zcash Community Forum before the new seats are filled.

Until the new seats are filled, the ZCG Committee continues to operate with its existing Members, and the quorum and voting rules in this ZIP apply with five in place of seven seats: the quorum is four, and a Large Grant requires three votes in favor.

#Quorum and voting

A decision of the ZCG Committee MUST NOT be made unless at least five Members who are not recused take part.

A decision is approved by a majority of the votes cast by Members who are not recused.

A Large Grant MUST NOT be approved unless at least four Members vote in favor.

If recusals or vacancies mean that fewer than five Members are able to take part in a decision, the decision MAY be made by all Members who are able to take part, provided that there are at least four of them, and provided that a Large Grant still receives at least four votes in favor. The ZCG Committee MUST record in its published minutes when a decision is made under this paragraph, and why.

The ZCG Committee MUST publish, for each grant decision, the number of Members who voted in favor, against, and abstained, and the number who recused.

#Compensation

Compensation for the new seats SHALL be determined by ZCAP or successor process, and paid from the Discretionary Budget, under item 9 of the section "Zcash Community Grants (ZCG)" of ZIP 1015, in the same way as for the existing seats. ZCAP SHOULD review the size of the Discretionary Budget to take account of the additional seats.

#Changes to ZIP 1015

In the section "Zcash Community Grants (ZCG)" of ZIP 1015 5:

  • in item 5, "a five-seat ZCG Committee" is replaced with "a seven-seat ZCG Committee";
  • in item 7, the sentence on associations is replaced as specified in Association limits; and
  • at the end of item 5, the following is added:

The ZCG Committee's quorum, voting rules, and the transition from five to seven seats are specified in ZIP [this ZIP].

#Effective date

This ZIP takes effect on the date its Status becomes Active.

#Rationale

#Why seven

Seven is the smallest odd number that leaves a clear independent majority under the association limits in this ZIP: with at most three associated Members, at least four are independent. It also means that one recusal still leaves six Members, and two leave five, so a quorum can almost always be met.

Larger committees, such as nine or eleven, would further reduce the weight of each Member, but would be harder to fill with qualified people each year, cost more, and make discussion slower.

#Why extend the association limits

The limits in ZIP 1015 name BP/ECC, ZF, and FPF. Since then, ECC's former team has formed ZODL, and the Key-Holder Organizations have taken on administration of the Coinholder-Controlled Fund. ZODL shares the BP/ECC limit because its people were the ECC team when that limit was written. Adding organizations one at a time would erode the independent majority, so the total is capped at three.

#Why a four-vote floor for Large Grants

Under a simple majority of those voting, a Large Grant could pass with three votes if several Members were recused or absent. Requiring four votes means that every large decision is backed by a majority of the full Committee. The US$500,000 threshold is meant to catch the grants that matter most to ZCG's budget while leaving routine grants unaffected.

#Why keep the existing selection process

How the Committee is selected is a separate and more contested question. This ZIP only changes its size, so that it can be adopted without settling that question. It works with any selection process that ZIP 1015 or a later ZIP specifies.

#Relationship to other drafts

This ZIP fits with the drafts that bar Members from applying for grants during their term 7 and that move treasury decisions to a separate Treasury Committee 8. Neither depends on this one. If the Treasury Committee draft is adopted, the grants committee will spend its time on grant review, which makes the extra seats more useful.

#Open questions

  • Is US$500,000 the right threshold for a Large Grant, and should it be set in ZEC instead?
  • Should the quorum be five, or a majority of Seated Members?
  • Should the new seats be reserved, at least initially, for people with no association with BP/ECC, ZODL, ZF, FPF, a Key-Holder Organization, or any current grantee?
  • Should this ZIP also add a rule for filling a mid-term vacancy?
