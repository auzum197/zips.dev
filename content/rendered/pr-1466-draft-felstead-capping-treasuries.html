#Terminology

The key words "MUST", "SHALL", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT" and "MAY" in this document are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14 1 when, and only when, they appear in all capitals.

The terms "Zcash Community Grants" (or "ZCG"), "ZCG Committee" and "Deferred Dev Fund Lockbox" are to be interpreted as defined in ZIP 1015 2.

The terms "Coinholder-Controlled Fund" (or "CCF") and "Key-Holder Organizations" are to be interpreted as defined in ZIP 1016 3.

"NSM" means the Network Sustainability Mechanism 4 5.

"Programme" means either ZCG or the coinholder-directed grants programme funded by the Coinholder-Controlled Fund.

"Spendable Treasury" means the funds a Programme can currently spend: for ZCG, its treasury; for the CCF, the funds held by the Key-Holder Organizations on its behalf, including those disbursed to them from the Deferred Dev Fund Lockbox under ZIP 271 6. It does not include funds in any lockbox that can only be released by a consensus change. The CCF's 12% stream continues to accrue to the Deferred Development Lockbox, so it is not part of the CCF's Spendable Treasury until it is released.

"Measurement Date" means the last day of March, June, September and December.

"Reference Price" means the lower of the time-weighted average price of ZEC in USD over the 182 days ending on a Measurement Date and the average over the final 7 of those days, calculated as specified below.

"Committed Obligations" means payments a Programme has already approved but not yet made, such as approved grants and milestones. It also includes the total requested in any funding round whose vote has opened but not yet closed, so that no transfer can pre-empt a decision already under way.

"Near-Term Obligations" means the part of a Programme's Committed Obligations due to be paid within 24 months of a Measurement Date.

"Annual Spend" means the average yearly amount a Programme disbursed over the 24 months ending on a Measurement Date, or over its full history if that is shorter, valued in USD at the time of each payment.

"Minimum Reserve" means USD 25,000,000 for ZCG and USD 37,500,000 for the coinholder-directed grants programme, in the same 8:12 ratio as their funding streams.

"Justified Reserve" means a Programme's Committed Obligations plus the greater of its Minimum Reserve and two years of its Annual Spend less its Near-Term Obligations.

"Measured Value" means the value of a Programme's Spendable Treasury on a Measurement Date, calculated as specified below.

"Excess" means the amount by which a Programme's Measured Value exceeds its Justified Reserve.

"Transfer Cap" means, for each Measurement Date, 50,000 ZEC for ZCG and 75,000 ZEC for the coinholder-directed grants programme. 50,000 ZEC is the one-time transfer proposed for ZCG in the Future Dev and Legal Defense Fund ZIP 7; 75,000 ZEC is the same amount in the 8:12 ratio of the funding streams.

#Abstract

This ZIP applies one test to both grant programmes funded by the Zcash Development Fund.

Each quarter, each programme works out what it needs: what it has already committed to pay, plus a reserve based on its own spending, with a minimum in proportion to its funding stream. Its spendable treasury is valued in USD at a six-month time-weighted average price, or a recent average if lower. Anything above what it needs is sent to the Network Sustainability Mechanism, up to a cap of 50,000 ZEC for ZCG and 75,000 ZEC for the coinholder programme each quarter.

The test is the same for both programmes. Because their commitments and spending differ, the amounts they may keep will differ too.

This ZIP does not change either funding stream, their expiry in November 2028, or how either programme decides what to fund.

#Motivation

ZIP 1016 created two funds from the block subsidy until the third halving in November 2028: 8% to Zcash Community Grants, and 12% to the Coinholder-Controlled Fund.

The split dates from the first Dev Fund. ZIP 1014 8 divided 20% of the block subsidy between the Electric Coin Company (7%), the Zcash Foundation (5%) and Major Grants (8%), the programme now called ZCG. When that Dev Fund ended in 2024, ZIP 1015 kept 8% for ZCG and sent the remaining 12% to a lockbox, to encourage the development of a decentralised way of allocating it. ZIP 1016 kept the same 8% and 12%, and directed the 12% to the Coinholder-Controlled Fund. The ratio reflects how the original 20% was divided, not an assessment of what either programme needs.

The ZEC price has since risen sharply. Both funds now receive far more in USD than anyone expected when ZIP 1016 was adopted. Several proposals have responded by changing ZCG's stream and treasury, including a fixed transfer of ZEC to the NSM regardless of price. None applies the same test to the Coinholder-Controlled Fund, which receives the larger stream.

ZCG itself first proposed reducing the funding of both programmes, with the difference going to the NSM, then withdrew the proposal so that the community could decide through Zcash's governance process 9.

If a higher price makes 8% too much, it makes 12% too much as well. Neither programme should accumulate more money than it can justify. The question is not which programme keeps its money, but how much each needs, judged the same way.

Equal scrutiny does not mean identical limits. The two programmes have different mandates, commitments and spending patterns. This ZIP applies one method to both, and lets the numbers differ where the facts differ.

One of the aims is governance minimisation. Less to decide, less to fight over. A published formula, applied the same way every quarter, replaces repeated one-off debates about the size of each treasury.

#Requirements

  • The same method, price measurement and schedule apply to both Programmes.
  • Work a Programme has already approved is protected.
  • A Programme always keeps enough to pay its Committed Obligations and two years of its own spending, never less than its Committed Obligations plus its Minimum Reserve, and gives up less as the price falls.
  • The minimum reserves are in the same 8:12 ratio as the funding streams, preserving the balance ZIP 1016 struck.
  • The calculation can be checked by anyone using public data.
  • Excess leaves the Programmes and returns to the protocol, not to any organisation or constituency.
  • Funds in a lockbox are not moved automatically.
  • Neither Programme's governance, grant process or funding stream is changed.

#Non-requirements

  • Any change to the amount or duration of the ZCG or Coinholder-Controlled Fund funding streams defined in ZIP 1016.
  • Any change to how either Programme decides what to fund.
  • Any change to who elects the ZCG Committee or who votes on coinholder-directed grants.
  • Deciding what happens to either Programme after November 2028.

#Specification

#Measured Value

On each Measurement Date, each Programme's Measured Value SHALL be calculated as:

Measured Value = (ZEC in the Spendable Treasury × Reference Price)
               + (USD and USD-denominated stablecoins held, at face value)
               + (other assets held, at market value)

#Justified Reserve

Each Programme's Justified Reserve SHALL be calculated as:

Justified Reserve = Committed Obligations
                  + max(Minimum Reserve,
                        2 × Annual Spend − Near-Term Obligations)

Near-Term Obligations are subtracted so that payments already committed are not counted twice: once as Committed Obligations, and again as part of the next two years of spending.

The same formula SHALL be used for both Programmes.

#Reference Price

For each day, the daily price is the median ZEC/USD closing price across at least three major exchanges with public price data.

The Reference Price SHALL be the lower of:

  1. the mean of the daily prices over the 182 days ending on the Measurement Date; and
  2. the mean of the daily prices over the 7 days ending on the Measurement Date.

The administrators of both Programmes SHALL agree and publish the list of exchanges before the first Measurement Date. The same Reference Price SHALL be used for both Programmes.

#Excess

If a Programme's Measured Value exceeds its Justified Reserve, the Excess, converted to ZEC at the Reference Price, SHALL be sent to the NSM, up to that Programme's Transfer Cap. Any Excess above the Transfer Cap remains in the Spendable Treasury and is measured again on the next Measurement Date.

#Reporting and transfer

Within 60 days of each Measurement Date, the administrators of each Programme SHALL:

  1. publish the calculation of its Measured Value and Justified Reserve, with enough detail for anyone to check it, listing its Committed Obligations individually where they are already public and otherwise in aggregate; and
  2. if there is any Excess, and transfers are possible under the Deployment section, send the amount due under the Excess section to the NSM in one or more transactions, and publish their transaction IDs.

For ZCG, the administrators are the organisation administering its treasury. For the CCF, they are the Key-Holder Organizations.

For the CCF, the calculation SHALL also report the balance of any unreleased lockbox funds allocated to it. These remain CCF funds, but are reported separately and excluded from its Measured Value.

The calculation MAY be published as part of a Programme's regular quarterly report. If there is no Excess, publishing the calculation is all that is required.

#Holdings in USD

Each Programme SHOULD hold, in the currency in which the payments are owed:

  1. its Committed Obligations; and
  2. its expected spending over the next 12 months that is not already a Committed Obligation, estimated from its Annual Spend.

Amounts owed in USD SHOULD be held in USD or USD-denominated stablecoins, where the Programme's administrators can lawfully do so. Amounts owed in ZEC MAY be held in ZEC.

These holdings are part of the Justified Reserve, not an addition to it. They provide the liquidity to pay what falls due. They count towards the Measured Value at face value, or, for ZEC, at the Reference Price.

This builds on ZCG's existing practice of converting approved grant amounts to USD, and of aiming to keep around 10% of its treasury in USD 10. Basing the holding on what each Programme owes, when and in which currency, rather than on a percentage, keeps it meaningful as the ZEC price moves, and fits each Programme's own payment pattern 11.

Each Programme's published calculation need only state the USD value of these holdings. How and where they are held MAY be kept confidential, and SHOULD be made available to the independent checker described under Privacy and security considerations.

#Lockboxes

Funds in the Deferred Dev Fund Lockbox, or in any other lockbox that can only be released by a consensus change, are not part of a Spendable Treasury and SHALL NOT be moved automatically under this ZIP.

Any future ZIP that releases lockbox funds to a Programme SHALL apply the test in this ZIP to the amount released, so that no release takes a Programme above its Justified Reserve.

#Expiry

This ZIP applies from its acceptance until the ZCG and Coinholder-Controlled Fund funding streams defined in ZIP 1016 end in November 2028. What happens after that is left to a future ZIP.

#Rationale

#What this ZIP is for

This ZIP does not aim to maximise transfers to the NSM. The ZCG stream is intended "to fund independent teams entering the Zcash ecosystem, to perform major ongoing development (or other work) for the public good of the Zcash ecosystem" 2. The Coinholder-Controlled Fund "may be used to distribute larger grants to ecosystem participants, or left at rest" 3. Spending on those purposes is what each Programme is for. This ZIP only removes funds that are excess to a Programme's needs at the Reference Price.

ZIP 1016 also says that coinholders are not obligated to fund any grants and MAY leave the funds at rest. This ZIP keeps that freedom for everything up to the Justified Reserve: a Programme's Committed Obligations plus at least two years of its own spending, or its minimum reserve if that is higher. Only funds above that are sent to the NSM. To that extent, this ZIP updates ZIP 1016.

#Why the same test for both programmes

The concern raised about ZCG is that its treasury is growing faster than it can usefully be spent, at a price nobody anticipated. That concern applies at least as strongly to the Coinholder-Controlled Fund, which receives 12% of the block subsidy to ZCG's 8%.

A limit that applies to one programme and not the other is not a response to treasury size. It shifts influence between constituencies. This ZIP takes no position on which constituency should control more funding. It only requires that both be judged the same way.

#Why not identical limits

The two programmes are different. One funds work in advance; the other rewards work already done. Their commitments, payment schedules and risks differ. Identical limits would treat different things as if they were the same.

The fair approach is one method, applied to each programme's own facts. The amounts may differ, but every difference can be traced to a published number.

#Why a reserve based on spending, with a minimum

A reserve of two years of actual spending lets each programme keep working through a price fall without cutting grants already planned.

The minimum protects a programme whose spending is low or uneven, such as one that pays grants retroactively. Without it, a quiet year would force a programme to give up the funds it needs for the next.

The minimums are USD 25 million for ZCG, the illustrative figure suggested in the current Dev Fund discussion 12, and USD 37.5 million for the coinholder-directed programme. They are in the same 8:12 ratio as the funding streams. Equal minimums would quietly shift the balance ZIP 1016 struck towards ZCG. Proportional minimums leave it where it is. This ZIP does not reopen the 8:12 split.

Both figures are starting points for discussion. The 8:12 ratio keeps the balance between the programmes, but it does not by itself show what either needs. Open question 1 asks for evidence on that.

Spending is averaged over two years, so that one exceptional grant or a delayed payment does not distort the reserve in either direction. Payments already committed and due within those two years are counted once, as Committed Obligations, and not again as future spending 11.

#Why a cap of 50,000 ZEC

The Future Dev and Legal Defense Fund ZIP proposes that ZCG transfer 50,000 ZEC to the NSM once, whatever the price. This ZIP takes that figure as the most either programme gives up in any quarter, scaled 8:12 for the coinholder programme.

So this ZIP is never harsher on ZCG in a single quarter than that proposal, and is gentler when the price falls. Because the test repeats each quarter, a treasury that is still above its reserve continues to be trimmed, gradually, rather than growing again.

#Why a six-month average price

Both programmes pay for work priced in USD. A limit in ZEC would swing with the price, as a fixed transfer of ZEC does: 50,000 ZEC is about USD 65 million at USD 1,300, and USD 2.5 million at USD 50.

A six-month time-weighted average smooths out short-term spikes and makes the measurement harder to manipulate.

It also lags. After a sharp fall, the six-month average will be higher than the market price, so a Programme would be treated as richer than it is and could be left below its Justified Reserve. Using the lower of the six-month average and the average of the last seven days prevents this. A recent spike cannot trigger a transfer either, because the six-month average is then the lower figure. A seven-day average, rather than a single day's price, makes the recent figure harder to push down to avoid a transfer.

#Why lockboxes are not moved automatically

Lockbox funds can only move through a consensus change, and are not available to either programme today. Treating them as spendable would overstate what a programme holds. Instead, this ZIP requires that any future release apply the same test, so lockbox funds cannot be used to get around it.

#Why the NSM

Sending the excess to the NSM takes it out of circulation and returns it to the protocol, where it is reissued over time. No organisation gains from it.

The NSM does not start reissuing funds straight away, so this is not about paying miners sooner. It is about where excess goes: back to the protocol, not to any organisation.

Sending excess to the NSM has costs, and Ian Miers has set them out 13. The NSM could become a reserve that a later upgrade redirects. It adds to issuance from 2031, which complicates Zcash's issuance story. And it ultimately pays miners. These costs are real. But redirecting NSM funds needs a consensus change, the highest bar Zcash has, while ZEC held in a treasury can be spent by whoever controls it. The alternatives are listed as an open question.

The same post proposes capping how much any one grantee can receive from the dev fund. That addresses a different risk, and would complement this ZIP.

#Why the test ends in November 2028

The test exists because both funding streams keep adding to treasuries faster than they can usefully be spent. When the streams end in November 2028, nothing new flows in. A treasury can then only shrink as it is spent, and each Programme will need what it holds to keep funding work until any successor arrangement is agreed. Continuing to take funds away after that point would risk leaving a Programme short, with no income to replace them.

#Why quarterly, and why no consensus change

Both Programmes already report and run funding rounds quarterly, so the test can reuse figures they already produce. Sixty days after each Measurement Date allows the calculation to be published with the regular quarterly report.

A consensus change would take longer, add code to every node implementation, and be harder to adjust. The calculation needs none. Transfers need a way to remove funds from circulation, which ZIP 233 would provide, but this ZIP does not itself change the protocol. It places obligations on the organisations that administer each Programme, in the same way as ZIP 1015 and ZIP 1016 already do. It can be changed by a later ZIP if the figures prove wrong.

If transfers are delayed, a Programme does not keep its excess. The test measures the treasury balance, so any excess still held when transfers become possible is measured and sent in the usual way, subject to the Transfer Cap.

#ZIP 233 and NU7.1

ZIP 233 4 provides a general-purpose mechanism for voluntarily removing ZEC from circulation into the NSM. It depends on the extensible transaction format in ZIP 248 14.

The published timeline for NU7.1 places transaction-format changes outside its scope 15. nuttycom has proposed activating ZIP 248 in any network upgrade that follows the current polling, partly because it would enable ZIP 233 15. Transfers under this ZIP depend on that mechanism, as does the one-time transfer proposed in the Future Dev and Legal Defense Fund ZIP. Any decision to deploy it should depend on complete specifications, implementation readiness and thorough technical review.

#Interaction with other proposals

This ZIP can be adopted alone or alongside other proposals for the Dev Fund. If another ZIP transfers funds out of a Programme or ends a funding stream, this ZIP applies to whatever remains in that Programme's Spendable Treasury.

In the falling scenario, the price falls to USD 750, 500, 300 and then 200 over the first four quarters, and stays at USD 200.

At a steady price, this ZIP takes more out of ZCG than a fixed transfer, because it removes everything above the Justified Reserve, and ZCG ends with that reserve. The difference is in a fall. This ZIP stops transfers as the price drops, and ZCG keeps more than two years of runway. A fixed transfer, with the stream redirected, cannot adjust: ZCG runs out of ZEC in the sixth quarter, and ends with about half the USD it needs for its commitments and next year's spending.

The hardest case for this ZIP is a strong market followed by a sharp fall. If the price rises to USD 1,900 over two quarters and then falls to USD 300, this ZIP sends about 100,000 ZEC in the first two quarters. ZCG then falls to about USD 18 to 19 million, below its USD 25 million minimum, for about a year, until its stream rebuilds it. Its commitments are still met, and its runway does not fall below two years, but transfers already made cannot be undone 11. Open question 9 considers a fix.

The coinholder-directed programme is not shown, because its 12% stream accrues to the Deferred Dev Fund Lockbox rather than its Spendable Treasury, and its spendable holdings need to be confirmed first.

#Privacy and security considerations

The calculation uses only aggregate balances, public price data, total spending and approved commitments, which each programme already publishes. It needs no private information about individual grant recipients.

Committed Obligations are listed individually only where they are already public. Others, such as security bounties whose recipients have not been named, are given in aggregate, with the detail available to the independent checker described below.

Sending ZEC to the NSM is irreversible. Each published calculation SHOULD be checked by at least one party independent of the administering organisation before any transfer is made. Transfers to the NSM are visible on chain, so anyone can confirm that the amount sent matches the published calculation.

Each transfer requires the administrators to use their signing keys, and for the CCF to coordinate a multisig signature. This adds a few signing events each year to what the Programmes already do. Administrators SHOULD use the same key management procedures as for grant payments.

A Programme could try to avoid a transfer by approving grants or raising its spending just before a Measurement Date. Publishing the calculation, listing Committed Obligations, and basing the reserve on a full year of actual spending make this visible and limit its effect.

Funds that keep flowing to a Programme are held under its administrators' keys, whereas funds in a protocol lockbox are held by no one until they are released. This ZIP limits that exposure in a different way: each quarter, holdings above the Justified Reserve leave the Programme's keys for the NSM. This applies to both Programmes, so it bounds the amount held by the Key-Holder Organizations as well as by ZCG's administrator, and it avoids a later release in which a large lockbox balance moves into keys in a single transaction.

Prices are taken across several exchanges. Pushing the price up cannot trigger a transfer, because the lower of the two averages is used, and would benefit no individual party in any case. Pushing the seven-day average down to avoid a transfer would mean holding the price down across several exchanges for a week; it would be costly and visible, and would only delay the transfer to the following quarter's measurement.

#Deployment

This ZIP needs no consensus change. It SHALL take effect on the first Measurement Date at least 30 days after it is accepted, so that the list of exchanges can be agreed and published first.

The quarterly calculation applies from that date. Transfers SHALL begin only once ZIP 233 4, or another independently approved general-purpose mechanism for removing funds from circulation into the NSM, is active and the administrators are authorised to use it. Until then, the calculation is published but no transfer is made, and no catch-up transfers accumulate.

#Open questions

  1. Is two years of spending the right reserve, and are USD 25 million and USD 37.5 million the right minimums? The 8:12 ratio keeps the balance between the programmes, but does not by itself show what either needs. What evidence should set the minimums?
  2. Should the Transfer Cap apply per quarter, as here, or in total?
  3. What counts as a Committed Obligation? For example, should approved operating costs be included?
  4. Which exchanges should set the Reference Price, and who checks the calculation?
  5. Is quarterly the right frequency?
  6. Is 12 months the right horizon for expected new spending held in the currency it is owed in, or should it be longer? bloxster has suggested holding two to three years of spending in USD.
  7. Should excess go to the NSM, as here, be burned permanently, or stay locked until a later decision?
  8. Is 24 months the right period for averaging spending? The coinholder-directed programme has a shorter history, so its average will rest on less data at first. Its spending is also lumpy: FPF reports that 98% of its payments to date went out on a single day, after the third-quarter 2026 round. While a Programme's minimum applies, its average does not change the result.
  9. Should the test value ZEC at half the Reference Price when deciding whether there is an Excess? In a strong market followed by a sharp fall, transfers made at the high price can leave a Programme below its minimum after the fall. Testing at half price would still send most of the Excess when prices are high, but in the illustration it raises ZCG's low point after a crash from about USD 18 million to about USD 21 million, and after a fall to USD 200 from about USD 17 million to about USD 23 million.
