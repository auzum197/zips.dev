#Terminology

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "SHALL", and "SHOULD" in this document are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14 1 when, and only when, they appear in all capitals.

"Zcash Community Grants" ("ZCG"), "ZCG Committee", "Zcash Community Advisory Panel" ("ZCAP"), and "Financial Privacy Foundation" ("FPF") have the meanings given in ZIP 1015, subject to the amendment to ZCG's definition below. 2

"Coinholder-Controlled Fund" ("CCF") has the meaning given in ZIP 1016. 3 "ZEC" is the native currency of Zcash Mainnet.

"Coinholder election" means an election in which eligible ZEC provides voting weight, rather than membership in ZCAP. "Approval voting" means that a voter can support any number of candidates and the candidates with the highest approval totals fill the available seats, subject to committee eligibility constraints.

"Effective Date" means the UTC date specified for the transition under [Activation and transition]. This date is unassigned in this draft.

#Abstract

This ZIP changes the electorate that selects the Zcash Community Grants Committee. ZEC holders would elect committee members through private, ZEC-weighted approval voting, replacing elections by the Zcash Community Advisory Panel. The committee would continue to evaluate and approve grants under its existing mandate.

The proposal preserves the five-seat committee, staggered elections, one-year terms, eligibility for reelection, conflict-of-interest rules, and existing candidate nomination process. It changes neither ZCG's funding allocation nor the use of its treasury. FPF would continue administering the program under the existing financial, legal, and reporting constraints. ZCAP's separate roles in determining the Discretionary Budget and committee compensation would remain unchanged.

This ZIP specifies amendments to ZIPs 1015 and 1016 and requires corresponding amendments to FPF's legal commitments so that the election results govern committee selection under the program's binding arrangements. Publication or adoption of a ZIP alone does not amend an existing agreement.

The voting mechanism is outside the scope of this proposal. The remaining election rules and rollout details are to be completed before activation. It introduces no changes to Zcash consensus rules or transaction privacy. Election privacy nevertheless depends on the voting system, its operators, and the information exposed during participation and tallying.

#Motivation

ZIP 1015 provides for a five-seat ZCG Committee selected by ZCAP or a successor process. ZIP 1016 continues this arrangement while providing a separate coinholder-directed funding channel. 4 3

In the June 2026 ZCG election, ZCAP used approval voting to fill two seats. Voters could approve multiple candidates, and the two candidates with the most approvals were elected. 5 This gives members of an advisory panel the formal role of selecting the committee, while ZEC ownership alone provides no participation right in that election.

ZEC holders have an economic interest in the long-term effects of development funding. Giving them responsibility for electing ZCG members would connect that interest to the selection of the people who allocate ZCG resources. It would also allow participation without admission to a membership panel. The proposal retains a representative committee, allowing holders to choose its members without requiring a coinholder vote on each ZCG grant.

ZIP 1016 leaves the definition of the coinholder voting model to a separate ZIP. 3 No Active ZIP currently specifies a coinholder voting mechanism for these elections. This proposal defines the election rules and leaves the voting technology to separate work.

The change is deliberately limited to committee selection. It does not transfer ZCG's grant decisions to the CCF or change the committee's duties, budget authority, or FPF's administration of grants.

#Privacy Implications

ZEC-weighted voting can expose financial information if eligibility proofs or ballots reveal balances, addresses, or links between holdings and choices. This proposal calls for private ballots and independently verifiable aggregate results. How the voting system protects individual balances and choices depends on its design and operational assumptions.

Small participation sets, distinctive voting weights, repeated elections, and network metadata can still create inference or correlation risks. A private ballot also does not by itself establish resistance to coercion or vote buying. These risks need independent review in the context of the voting system used for ZCG elections.

Candidates would continue to participate through the existing nomination and disclosure process. This ZIP does not alter Zcash transaction formats, shielded payment privacy, or the information grantees provide to FPF.

#Requirements

  • Replace ZCAP membership with eligible ZEC as the basis for voting in ZCG committee elections, using private, verifiable coinholder voting.
  • Preserve approval voting and the existing committee structure, nomination process, election cadence, term lengths, and eligibility constraints.
  • Preserve ZCG's grant mandate, funding, treasury, administration, and all governance responsibilities other than the election of committee members.
  • Make the election outcome binding on FPF through explicit amendments to ZIPs 1015 and 1016 and the corresponding legal commitments.
  • Protect voter balances and choices while allowing independent verification of the tally and its application to the available seats.
  • Transition prospectively, without shortening or extending existing terms or reopening completed elections.

#Specification

The following requirements govern ZCG committee selection and its legal implementation. They do not change Zcash consensus rules.

#Electorate and ballot

From the Effective Date, ZCG Committee members MUST be selected by coinholder election instead of a ZCAP election or another membership-panel election. FPF MUST arrange the election, with technical and administrative support as needed. ZCAP membership MUST NOT be a condition of voter eligibility. Any individual or organization controlling eligible ZEC MUST be permitted to vote; the association limits on committee members do not restrict voters.

Elections MUST use ZEC-weighted approval voting. For each candidate, the ballot MUST allow approval or non-approval. A voter MUST be permitted to approve any number of candidates, including none. The same eligible voting weight MUST be usable independently for each candidate; it MUST NOT be divided by the number of candidates approved.

Each candidate's score is the sum of the valid ZEC voting weight approving that candidate. In an election that meets quorum, available seats MUST be filled in descending order of score, subject to the eligibility and association limits in point 7 of ZIP 1015's "Zcash Community Grants (ZCG)" section, including associations of members whose terms continue. Approval of a candidate does not require a separate majority of the participating weight.

An election MUST meet the minimum ZEC participation threshold in force for CCF grant votes under ZIP 1016 when voting opens. 3 Turnout is the total eligible ZEC represented by valid ballots. The same ZEC MUST be counted only once toward turnout, regardless of how many candidates it approves. An election that falls short of quorum MUST NOT fill any seats.

#Election administration and record

Elections MUST protect individual choices and balances and allow independent verification of the aggregate tally. Elections MUST follow any applicable Active ZIPs governing ZEC eligibility, voting weight, ballot validation, or tally verification.

When FPF opens nominations, it MUST announce the voting window, number of seats, and candidate eligibility rules. FPF MUST publish the voter eligibility rules and voting instructions in time for voters to prepare before any eligibility cutoff and participate in the vote. The final candidate list and its mapping to the ballot MUST be published before voting opens. The election record MUST include the quorum threshold, turnout, and verified aggregate tally. It MUST explain how the quorum and candidate eligibility rules determined the outcome.

Tie resolution and procedures for an invalid or interrupted election or an election that falls short of quorum remain open in this draft. These rules MUST be resolved in this ZIP before it becomes Active, including how vacancies are handled without changing the existing one-year terms or granting FPF discretion to appoint unelected candidates.

#Activation and transition

This ZIP MUST NOT take effect while its status is Draft. Before activation, the ZIP process MUST establish community adoption, this ZIP MUST specify an Effective Date, and FPF MUST publish confirmation that the legal requirements below have been satisfied.

The new electorate MUST apply to elections whose nomination period opens on or after the Effective Date, including elections to fill vacancies. An election already underway before that date MUST finish under its announced rules. Completed elections and the terms of sitting members are unaffected.

The five-seat committee, staggered election schedule, one-year terms, eligibility for reelection, candidate nomination process, conflict-of-interest requirements, and association limits MUST continue. Except for replacing the electorate and implementing coin weighting and ballot privacy as specified here, existing election procedures MUST continue.

This is a change to a community process. Activation does not require a network upgrade or changes to ZIP 214's funding-stream definitions. A rehearsal SHOULD be held before the first election. Rehearsals MUST NOT select ZCG Committee members or confer authority over ZCG funds.

#Legal implementation by FPF

FPF MUST arrange amendments, in suitable legal language, to the restricted-fund agreements and other binding commitments through which it implements ZIPs 1015 and 1016. The amendments MUST require FPF to recognize the candidates elected under this ZIP as the ZCG Committee members for the relevant seats and terms, subject to the existing eligibility requirements and applicable law. A separate ZCAP ratification MUST NOT be required.

Before the Effective Date, FPF MUST obtain advice from qualified Cayman Islands counsel on the necessary amendments, consents, and corporate approvals, and complete those steps. The binding instruments MUST identify the parties entitled to enforce the election obligations and the applicable enforcement procedure. This ZIP does not itself confer legal standing on every ZEC holder or automatically amend an agreement or FPF's constitutional documents.

FPF MUST publish confirmation of the completed legal arrangements and their effect on committee selection, protecting privileged advice and confidential information. If a necessary authorization cannot be obtained, FPF MUST report the obstacle and the transition MUST be deferred pending its resolution. Existing arrangements continue in the meantime; this does not authorize FPF to replace elections with discretionary appointments.

#Rationale

#Retaining a grants committee

Coinholder elections retain ZCG's existing division of work: an elected committee evaluates applications, and FPF administers the program. Direct coinholder voting on individual ZCG grants would be a broader redesign and would duplicate aspects of the CCF. This proposal changes who chooses the representatives while preserving what those representatives do.

#Preserving approval voting

Approval voting is already used for ZCG elections. 6 Weighting approvals by ZEC preserves the ability to support multiple candidates without splitting voting weight among them. The CCF's minimum participation threshold applies to the election as a whole. Once quorum is met, the approval totals determine the order in which candidates fill seats.

FPF published the June 2026 voting window when it opened nominations. 7 The same practice fits the staggered election calendar and gives FPF room to accommodate any voter eligibility cutoff.

#Concentration and participation

Coin weighting gives larger holders more influence, and a sufficiently large participating holder could determine all seats being contested. It does not provide one-person-one-vote representation or guarantee decentralization. Depending on the voting rules, temporarily borrowed balances can count and custodians may vote without reflecting each beneficial owner's preference. These are tradeoffs of the proposed electorate, not problems eliminated by ballot privacy. Public nominations, staggered terms, and existing committee constraints remain useful but do not remove those risks.

The applicable voting rules can limit which holdings qualify and how voters prepare to participate. Adequate notice matters to participation. Quorum and failure rules need to balance legitimacy against the risk of leaving seats unfilled.

#Express amendments and legal commitments

ZIP 1015 already permits a successor selection process, but leaves ZCAP selection as an option. An express amendment makes the replacement electorate unambiguous. Updating ZIP 1016 ensures that its continuation of ZIP 1015's obligations carries the same rule forward.

The legal arrangements are necessary to make FPF's obligation executable under the restricted-fund framework. The proposal retains the existing Enforcement provision; it does not implement automatic on-chain enforcement or alter funding streams. The distinction between process adoption and contractual implementation is essential to the intended binding effect.

#Open questions

  • How are tied scores at the seat boundary, zero-support candidates, insufficient eligible candidates, and elections that are interrupted or fall short of quorum handled while preserving the existing terms and cadence?
  • Which FPF agreements and corporate instruments need amendment, whose consent is required, and who can enforce the revised election obligations?
  • What Effective Date allows the legal work, independent security and privacy review, wallet preparation, and rehearsal to finish before nominations open?
